Getting started with crafts: a beginner’s buying guide

What you actually need to start crochet, embroidery, painting or clay — and the beginner mistakes that waste money.

    The fastest way to lose interest in a new craft is to buy a huge kit, get overwhelmed and never open half of it. Choose one craft, buy the small set of tools a beginner really needs, and only add more once you know you enjoy it.

    Choosing your first craft

    • Crochet — portable, cheap to start, and you can make useful things (coasters, bags, toys) within weeks.
    • Embroidery — relaxing, needs very little space, and turns plain cloth, bags and kurtas into something personal.
    • Acrylic painting — forgiving for beginners because acrylics dry fast and can be painted over.
    • Air-dry clay — no oven or kiln needed; good for trinket dishes, ornaments and kids’ projects.

    Crochet: what to buy

    Start with a 4 mm or 5 mm hook and a medium-weight (often labelled DK or worsted) cotton or acrylic yarn in a light, solid colour — dark and fluffy yarn makes it hard to see your stitches. Ergonomic hooks with soft handles are easier on the hands for long sessions. Add a tapestry needle and a pair of small scissors and you are ready.

    Embroidery: what to buy

    You need a 6-inch (15 cm) hoop, plain cotton fabric, a pack of embroidery needles and six-strand cotton floss in a handful of colours. A beginner kit with a pre-printed pattern is a good first project because it removes the hardest part — drawing the design.

    Painting: what to buy

    A student-grade acrylic set of 12 colours, three brushes (a small round, a medium flat and a larger flat), canvas boards or thick paper, and a palette are enough. Upgrade to artist-grade paints only once you are painting regularly — the difference matters less than practice.

    Clay: what to buy

    Air-dry clay, a few basic modelling tools, a rolling pin and a smooth work mat. Keep unused clay wrapped tightly in plastic so it does not dry out. Seal finished pieces with a clear varnish to protect them.

    Beginner tips that save money

    Follow one free video tutorial from start to finish before buying more supplies. Keep a small box for each craft so everything is in one place. And finish small projects — a finished coaster teaches more than an unfinished blanket.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which craft is cheapest to start?

    Crochet and embroidery both start with very small budgets, since one hook or hoop plus some yarn or floss is enough.

    Is resin art good for beginners?

    Resin can be rewarding but needs good ventilation, gloves and careful measuring. Try a simpler craft first if you are new.

    Are craft kits good for children?

    Yes, as long as the kit is labelled for the child’s age. Air-dry clay and simple embroidery kits are popular choices.
