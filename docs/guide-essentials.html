Everyday essentials checklist: what every home should keep stocked

The cleaning, safety and household basics that you only miss when they run out — and how to buy them smartly.

    Essentials are boring until the moment you need one and it is not there: a torch during a power cut, a bandage for a cut finger, a garbage bag on collection day. Use this checklist to stock up once, then top up as you go.

    Cleaning

    • Microfiber cloths — they lift dust instead of spreading it and can be washed hundreds of times.
    • A floor cleaner, a dishwash liquid and a multi-surface spray.
    • A refillable spray bottle so you can dilute concentrates.
    • Garbage bags in the right size for your bin — check the dimensions, not just the litre number.

    Safety and first aid

    • A basic first-aid kit: adhesive bandages, sterile gauze, antiseptic, medical tape, scissors, a digital thermometer and any regular medicines.
    • An LED torch or emergency light for power cuts.
    • A surge-protected extension board with the ISI mark for electronics.

    Check expiry dates in the first-aid kit every six months.

    Power and batteries

    Rechargeable AA and AAA batteries with a charger pay for themselves quickly in remotes, clocks and toys. Keep a few spare fuses or a small screwdriver set for quick fixes.

    Buy smart: compare unit prices

    Bigger packs are not always cheaper. Compare the price per 100 g, per litre or per piece before buying — many listings show this under the price. Buy non-perishables like garbage bags and detergent in bulk, but buy food and medicines only in quantities you will finish before they expire.

    Set up a simple restock system

    Keep a note on your phone. When you open the last pack of something, add it to the list. Subscribe-and-save options can help for things you buy on a predictable schedule.

    Frequently asked questions

    What should a basic home first-aid kit contain?

    Bandages, sterile gauze, antiseptic, medical tape, scissors, a digital thermometer and any medicines your family uses regularly.

    Is it cheaper to buy in bulk?

    Often, but not always. Compare the unit price, and only bulk-buy things that will not expire before you use them.

    Are rechargeable batteries worth it?

    For devices that use batteries often — remotes, toys, game controllers — yes. For smoke alarms and clocks, long-life alkaline cells are fine.
