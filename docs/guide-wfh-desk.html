A comfortable work-from-home desk: setup guide for small spaces

How to set up a desk that is kind to your back and eyes, even in a corner of a bedroom — with tips for power cuts.

    Hunching over a laptop on the bed is fine for an hour, but not for a working week. A few small changes to how you sit and what you look at prevent most neck, wrist and eye strain. You do not need a big desk or an expensive chair to get there.

    Get the screen to eye level

    The top of your screen should be roughly at or just below eye level, about an arm’s length away. With a laptop, that means raising it on a stand and using a separate keyboard and mouse. This single change makes the biggest difference to neck comfort.

    Chair and posture

    Your feet should rest flat on the floor with knees at about a right angle. If your chair has no lower-back support, a lumbar cushion helps. Keep elbows close to your body and wrists straight while typing. Stand up and move for a few minutes every half hour.

    Lighting

    Place your desk so the window is to the side, not behind the screen or behind you, to avoid glare. Add a desk lamp with neutral white light (around 4000K) for evening work. Follow the 20-20-20 habit: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

    Power cuts and internet

    A small DC mini-UPS for your Wi-Fi router can keep the internet running for a few hours during short outages, as long as your broadband line itself still works. Keep a charged power bank for your phone and, if your laptop supports USB-C charging, check whether a USB-C PD power bank can top it up.

    Keep it tidy

    Cable clips and a small desk organiser keep the space calm. If the desk is in a bedroom, put work things into a box at the end of the day — it helps your brain switch off.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need an expensive ergonomic chair?

    Not necessarily. A stable chair at the right height plus a lumbar cushion and a raised screen solves most problems.

    Will a mini-UPS work with any router?

    Check that the UPS output voltage matches your router’s adapter (commonly 12V) and that it includes the right connector.
