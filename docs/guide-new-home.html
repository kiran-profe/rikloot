Moving into a new home: a first-week essentials checklist

A calm, room-by-room checklist so your first days in a new home are about settling in, not running to the shop five times.

    Moving day is tiring, and the first night in a new place is harder when you cannot find a bulb, a charger or a towel. Pack one “first-night box”, then work through the rest room by room over the first week.

    Your first-night box

    • Phone chargers and a power bank.
    • A torch and a couple of spare bulbs.
    • A multi-socket extension board.
    • Toilet paper, soap, towels and a change of clothes.
    • Bedsheets and pillows.
    • Kettle, cups, tea or coffee, and a few snacks.
    • Basic medicines and a first-aid kit.
    • Scissors or a box cutter, and garbage bags.

    Day one: safety checks

    Find the main electrical switch and the water valve. Test every switch and socket. Check that taps, flushes and geysers work and there are no leaks. Change or add locks if keys have passed through many hands. Note the meter readings and take photos for your records.

    The kitchen

    Wipe shelves before unpacking, and line them if you like. Unpack the everyday things first — one pan, the pressure cooker, plates and cutlery — and leave the special-occasion items for later. Set up airtight containers for staples before you stock up.

    Bathroom and cleaning

    Keep a cleaning kit ready: microfiber cloths, a toilet brush, floor cleaner and a bucket and mop. Add hooks or an over-door rack for towels, and a small shelf or caddy for toiletries.

    Settle in over the week

    Do not buy furniture or décor in the first few days. Live in the space, notice where you naturally put things, then buy organisers that fit. Measure doorways and walls before ordering anything large.

    Frequently asked questions

    What should I check before signing for a rented flat?

    Water pressure, electrical points, any dampness or leaks, the condition of fittings, and that you have photographed everything for the rental agreement.

    When should I buy furniture?

    After a week or two of living in the space, once you know how you use each room. Always measure first.
