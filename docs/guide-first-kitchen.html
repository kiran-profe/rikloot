Setting up your first kitchen on a budget: the 12 things you actually need

Moving into a hostel, PG or your first flat? Here is the short list that lets you cook real meals without overspending.

    A first kitchen does not need to look like a showroom. With about a dozen well-chosen things you can make dal, rice, eggs, sabzi, tea and simple snacks every day. Start small, cook for a month, and only then add what you find yourself missing.

    Step 1: decide how you will cook

    If your flat has a gas connection, a two-burner stove is the cheapest way to cook. If gas is not allowed or not yet connected — common in PGs and new rentals — an induction cooktop is the safest choice. It heats only the vessel, switches off automatically, and is easy to carry when you move.

    Induction needs cookware with a flat, magnetic base. Test any vessel you already own with a fridge magnet: if it sticks firmly, it will work.

    The core twelve

    1. A 3-litre pressure cooker — dal, rice, rajma and even one-pot pulao.
    2. A non-stick or tri-ply frying pan — eggs, dosas and quick sabzis.
    3. A small kadai or deep pan.
    4. A saucepan or milk pan for tea, milk and noodles.
    5. An electric kettle — the most-used appliance in many small kitchens.
    6. One sharp chef’s knife and a chopping board.
    7. A ladle, a flat spatula and a slotted spoon.
    8. A peeler and a grater.
    9. Two or three airtight containers for rice, atta and dal.
    10. A small set of plates, bowls and glasses — steel lasts forever.
    11. A strainer.
    12. A small spice box so you are not opening ten packets for every meal.

    What to skip at first

    Hold off on a mixer-grinder, a toaster, a sandwich maker or a big dinner set until you know your routine. Many people find that an electric kettle and a pressure cooker cover most of what they cook in the first few months. Ready-made masalas and ginger-garlic paste save time until you have a grinder.

    Make a small space work

    Use the wall: a hook rail or magnetic strip frees counter space. Stack containers instead of spreading them out. Keep a damp cloth and a small bin next to where you cook so cleaning up takes a minute.

    Simple weekly routine

    Soak and cook a batch of dal or chana twice a week, keep onions, tomatoes, eggs and curd stocked, and you can put together a hot meal in 15 minutes on most days.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is induction cheaper to run than gas?

    It depends on your local electricity and LPG prices. Induction is very efficient because little heat is lost, so for small daily cooking the running cost is often similar.

    Can I use my old aluminium vessels on induction?

    Plain aluminium will not work. Use cookware marked induction compatible, or check with a magnet.
